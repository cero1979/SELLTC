# Novelty and literature audit

Search date: 2026-09-20. This is a focused, source-grounded review, not a proof that no related work exists. Searches covered classical LDPC/BEC peeling, stopping sets, hybrid ML decoding, certifying algorithms, proof-carrying code, formal error-correcting codes, SELL algorithm specifications, and recent certified-decoding/verification work. The exact-name search SELL + LDPC did not identify a closer publication; absence from those results is not a novelty theorem.

## Contribution-by-contribution assessment

| Proposed contribution | Closest verified antecedent | Already known | Defensible contribution of this artifact | Evidence and limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hybrid peeling plus algebraic decoding | Burshtein and Miller 2004; Richardson and Urbanke 2008 | Iterative erasure decoding followed by sparse/residual linear solving and BEC ML interpretation | A concrete instance to which certification is attached | No new decoder, threshold, error exponent or asymptotic decoding-speed theorem is claimed |
| Success/failure certificates | Certifying algorithms (McConnell et al. 2011); verified certifying computations (Alkassar et al. 2013); elementary linear algebra | Independent witness checking and linear infeasibility separators | Trace-plus-residual formats with explicit algebraic and operational contracts | Final parity/consistency can be checked without a trace; separators themselves are classical |
| SELL-style peeling specification | Nigam and Miller 2009; Andreoli 1992 | Resource-sensitive specifications, multiset state and focused proof search | A delayed-propagation invariant and explicit trace-labelled simulation for this peeling specification | The operational theorem is not a derivability/SELLF theorem; differentiated persistent zones are not essential here |
| Two persistent zones | Ordinary linear logic and standard read-and-recreate multiset rules | Reusable facts and separately tagged predicates | Readable division of immutable graph facts and acquired values | No expressiveness separation, necessity or new SELL metatheorem is justified |
| Formal correctness | Affeldt, Garrigue and Saikawa 2020; Alkassar et al. 2013 | Formal coding theory, extracted verified LDPC decoding, verified independent checkers | Whatever named theorem statements actually compile in the revised Lean artifact, with checked dependencies | Formal ECC is not new; Python is not automatically verified by an independently defined Lean function |
| Experimental certification overhead | Standard algorithm engineering and direct syndrome computation | Comparing checking costs with producer costs | Reproducible measurement of trace-validity cost relative to direct final-word checking, with actual ambiguities/corruptions | No general performance advantage is implied; hardware-specific finite measurements are not complexity proofs |

The substantive local logical work is the correspondence despite delayed propagation: each stored pending set contains all currently unknown neighbors and may also contain known neighbors awaiting propagation. This distinguishes the observable operational state from intermediate linear resources. The revised proof must establish arbitrary-run soundness and trace-preserving normalized completeness. Whether this application-specific theorem and artifact are a sufficiently significant advance for LMCS remains a matter for expert editorial judgment. The review does not certify journal readiness or priority.

## Primary sources checked and their precise roles

1. **Gallager (1962), Low-density parity-check codes**, IRE Transactions on Information Theory 8(1), 21–28. [IEEE publisher record](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/1057683/), DOI [10.1109/TIT.1962.1057683](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1962.1057683). Establishes the LDPC background; no novelty is assigned to sparse parity-check codes.
2. **Luby, Mitzenmacher, Shokrollahi and Spielman (2001), Efficient erasure correcting codes**, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 47(2), 569–584. Publisher-deposited Crossref metadata was obtained and is retained in `evidence/crossref-metadata.json`; DOI [10.1109/18.910575](https://doi.org/10.1109/18.910575). Used for iterative erasure decoding, not a claim about the new implementation.
3. **Di, Proietti, Telatar, Richardson and Urbanke (2002), Finite-length analysis of low-density parity-check codes on the binary erasure channel**, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 48(6), 1570–1579. Publisher-deposited metadata is in the same evidence file; DOI [10.1109/TIT.2002.1003839](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2002.1003839). Stopping sets are a classical obstruction. Direct IEEE full-text access encountered its browser challenge; no claim is made that the publisher PDF was fetched.
4. **Richardson and Urbanke (2008), Modern Coding Theory**, Cambridge University Press. [Publisher frontmatter/metadata](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/modern-coding-theory/frontmatter/F1304B645417CD0BBCFF27C5B49A8E6E), DOI [10.1017/CBO9780511791338](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511791338). Print year 2008 is distinguished from the 2012 online date. The contents explicitly include the BEC and iterative coding theory.
5. **Burshtein and Miller (2004), An efficient maximum-likelihood decoding of LDPC codes over the binary erasure channel**, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 50(11), 2837–2844. [Author-hosted full paper](https://www.eng.tau.ac.il/~burstyn/Publications/BEC_ML.pdf), [institutional publication record](https://cris.tau.ac.il/en/publications/an-efficient-maximum-likelihood-decoding-of-ldpc-codes-over-the-b/), DOI [10.1109/TIT.2004.836694](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2004.836694). It already extends iterative decoding with methods for sparse linear equations, discusses straightforward GE and structured alternatives, and relates stopping sets to iterative failure. This directly precludes presenting the hybrid algorithm itself as original.
6. **Hu, Eleftheriou and Arnold (2005), Regular and irregular progressive edge-growth Tanner graphs**, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 51(1), 386–398. [Full published paper](https://www.josephboutros.org/ldpc_vs_turbo/ldpc_Hu_ITjan05.pdf), DOI [10.1109/TIT.2004.839541](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2004.839541). The first PDF page shares space with the preceding paper; the PEG article begins on page 386. Standard PEG grows a graph by a distance/girth-sensitive choice. The original repository's different builder is called PEG-inspired; it inherits no published guarantee automatically.
7. **Girard (1987), Linear logic**, Theoretical Computer Science 50, 1–102. DOI [10.1016/0304-3975(87)90045-4](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(87)90045-4). Foundation for linear resources and ordinary exponentials. Indexing services disagree on an end page (101 versus 102); the retained 1–102 follows the original repository and standard author bibliographies, and is not used in any argument. Publisher primary full text was not accessible in this audit; foundational details used here are also available in the checked Andreoli and Nigam–Miller papers.
8. **Andreoli (1992), Logic programming with focusing proofs in linear logic**, Journal of Logic and Computation 2(3), 297–347. [Full paper](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~fp/courses/15816-s12/misc/andreoli92jlc.pdf), DOI [10.1093/logcom/2.3.297](https://doi.org/10.1093/logcom/2.3.297). A background source for focusing. Its general completeness result does not establish focusing adequacy for this encoding without a separate proof.
9. **Nigam and Miller (2009), Algorithmic specifications in linear logic with subexponentials**, PPDP, 129–140. [Author-hosted full paper](https://nigam.info/docs/ppdp11-nigam.pdf), [institutional record](https://researchportal.ip-paris.fr/en/publications/algorithmic-specifications-in-linear-logic-with-subexponentials/), DOI [10.1145/1599410.1599427](https://doi.org/10.1145/1599410.1599427). The paper supplies richer algorithmic uses of subexponentials, including location-sensitive tests. Our two persistent labels and two rules do not use those mechanisms. Do not conflate its proof-search result with the operational simulation established here.
10. **Necula (1997), Proof-carrying code**, POPL, 106–119. [ACM publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1145/263699.263712). Provides the separation between an untrusted producer and checked evidence. The present finite decoding witness is not an implementation of PCC's general code-safety proof system.
11. **Feldman, Wainwright and Karger (2005), Using linear programming to decode binary linear codes**, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 51(3), 954–972. [Author bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/jonfeld/pubs.html), [author-affiliated publication page](https://research.google/pubs/using-linear-programming-to-decode-binary-linear-codes/). Relevant to certification/optimality in decoding, but its LP relaxation and channel setting differ from checking a feasible BEC completion. The revised manuscript does not claim that decoder certification begins with this work.
12. **Affeldt, Garrigue and Saikawa (2020), A library for formalization of linear error-correcting codes**, Journal of Automated Reasoning 64(6), 1123–1164. [Author-hosted full preprint](https://staff.aist.go.jp/reynald.affeldt/documents/ecc.pdf), DOI [10.1007/s10817-019-09538-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10817-019-09538-8). It formalizes classical and modern coding theory in Coq and extracts a verified LDPC sum-product decoder. Its contribution is broader than checking parity equations and must not be reduced to elementary code definitions.
13. **McConnell, Mehlhorn, Näher and Schweitzer (2011), Certifying algorithms**, Computer Science Review 5(2), 119–161. [Author-affiliated institutional record](https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/certifying-algorithms/), DOI [10.1016/j.cosrev.2010.09.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cosrev.2010.09.009). Added because witness-producing algorithms and independent checkers are established methodology, not a new conceptual architecture here.
14. **Alkassar, Böhme, Mehlhorn and Rizkallah (2013), A framework for the verification of certifying computations**. [Author-deposited preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.7462), DOI [10.48550/arXiv.1301.7462](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1301.7462). Added because it explicitly connects mathematical witness properties with executable checker verification. The citation intentionally describes the inspected preprint, not invented final journal metadata.

## Search and metadata limitations

Searches for recent “certified decoding” also returned statistically calibrated receivers and quantum-code decoders; these use different meanings of certification and do not establish deterministic trace/witness checking for this classical BEC system. The 2026 search hit on conformal decode-or-erase is a statistical coverage result, not the same guarantee. No claim of a comprehensive literature exclusion is made.

An initial parallel Crossref title search was rate-limited (HTTP 429); several title searches also returned reviews rather than books. Those failures and unmatched hits remain in `evidence/crossref-metadata.json` and were not promoted into verified metadata. Bibliographic entries retained in `SELLTCFULL.bib` were instead grounded in publisher/institutional records and original papers as listed above. No DOI was invented to fill a missing field.

Current LMCS scope and formatting requirements are recorded separately in `EDITORIAL_INITIAL.md`. Topical fit is supported; sufficient novelty is not automatically established. Public archival release is still an author action because the repository is private and this task explicitly forbids publication without authorization.

## Search refresh on 23 September 2026

After resuming the task, a fresh search for LDPC/BEC certification and Lean/SELL verification identified two additional primary preprints. Their arXiv metadata and author-deposited HTML were opened directly; they are now cited as preprints, without invented journal or conference metadata:

- Mattias Ehatamm, Yi Lee, Xiaodi Wu and Runzhou Tao, *End-to-End Formalization of Quantum Error Correction*, arXiv:2605.16523v1, submitted 15 May 2026, DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2605.16523. [Primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16523v1). Its target is quantum-code distance via formal representation bridges and a SAT reduction. This is further evidence that Lean-based coding certificates are not a novelty claim in themselves; the present paper instead certifies BEC operational traces and completion/infeasibility. The comparison does not audit or endorse every proof in that external project.
- Ragavi Krishnamoorthy and ten coauthors, *Certified Decoding of Quantum LDPC Codes*, arXiv:2608.25545v1, submitted 26 August 2026, DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2608.25545. [Primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25545v1). Its certificates concern degenerate quantum-decoding optimality through partition-function comparisons, including statistical testing. These are different guarantees from exact binary feasibility/separation and peeling-trace validity. The revised related-work paragraph makes that distinction.

A search also surfaced a GitHub `j-arndt/qldpc` repository claiming Lean-checked two-sided quantum decoder certificates, but opening the primary URL returned 404. That inaccessible lead was not promoted to a verified bibliographic reference or used to establish any technical theorem. Search-result absence, presence or wording does not establish a priority result. The review remains focused, not an exhaustive proof that no earlier trace specification exists.
