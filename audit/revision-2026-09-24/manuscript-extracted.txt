Abstract. Certifying hybrid LDPC decoding over the binary erasure channel requires
connecting local peeling steps with a residual algebraic result. In an asynchronous speciﬁ-
cation, a solved variable may await propagation to some checks while other solves proceed,
so stored residuals need not match the operational state. We give a pending-incidence
invariant that accounts for this delay. Our principal theorem establishes exact trace ad-
equacy: every rewrite execution, with arbitrary interleaving of solving and propagation,
emits the same valid operational trace under projection; every prescribed valid trace has a
rewrite realization with its exact logged endpoint after propagation normalization. Empty
and partial traces are included. An abstract proposition isolates the simulation, canonical
normalization and local lifting hypotheses behind this correspondence. Residual linear
algebra then supplies independently checkable compatible-completion or left-kernel infea-
sibility certiﬁcates. Trace replay certiﬁes a local operational explanation in addition to the
ﬁnal-word predicate already established by direct checking. A Lean development mecha-
nizes the canonical LDPC correspondence and certiﬁcate results within stated boundaries;
ﬁnite tests and retained experiments assess an independent Python implementation and
its checking costs. The subexponential presentation exposes the resource discipline, while
an event-preserving translation shows that its two persistent labels are inessential for these
operational rules.
1. Introduction
Hybrid decoding over the binary erasure channel (BEC) combines local peeling with a linear
solve on the remaining erasures. An untrusted producer can accompany its result with a
certiﬁcate checked independently of that producer. The central question here is how an
asynchronous, resource-sensitive account of peeling can certify exactly the declared sequence
of local assignments when propagation is delayed. Our principal result, Theorem 6.10,
relates these rewrite executions to the same operational peeling traces in both directions
and identiﬁes their exact logged endpoints after normalization.
Four levels organize the construction. At the algorithmic level , classical peeling forces
erased variables and Gaussian elimination solves the residual system [ RU08, BM04]. At the
certiﬁcate level, witnesses separate the producer from trusted checking, following certifying
algorithms [ MMNS11, ABMR13, Nec97]. Here a successful witness completes the residual

Proposition 2.3 (Stopping sets) . A maximal peeling run leaves a nonempty unknown set
precisely when that remaining set is a stopping set. Peeling removes all initial erasures if
and only if the initial erasure set contains no stopping set. Here “removes” is a structural
statement, not acceptance of a codeword.
Proof. A step exists exactly when some check has one unknown neighbor. Consequently

a stopping subset of the initial unknown set. If peeling removed a member of S, consider
the ﬁrst such removal. At its witnessing check all members of S are still unknown; that
check touches S and hence has at least two unknown neighbors, contradicting degree one.
Thus S survives every run. Conversely, a nonempty residual of a maximal run is itself a
stopping subset of the initial erasure set, by the ﬁrst part. These implications establish
the characterization independently of the choice of eligible checks. A degree-zero row with
syndrome one may still rule out any completion; it does not change the structural argument.

The trusted instance is the supplied pair (H, y), including its validated graph repre-
sentation. The mathematical checker assumes exact binary arithmetic; an executable de-
ployment also trusts its parser, checker implementation, interpreter/compiler and runtime.
The decoder and its residual solver need not be trusted. A Lean proof of a mathematical
function does not by itself verify an independently written Python program or its execution
environment.
An accepted trace certiﬁes that the declared sequence is permitted by the speciﬁcation.
It does not attest that an untrusted producer actually followed this sequence internally.
A valid partial trace, or a permutation of independent steps that remains valid, must be
accepted. Malformed certiﬁcates, incompatible words and invalid steps must be rejected.
Rejection of a malformed failure witness alone does not establish that a completion exists.
If the supplied matrix is corrupted, acceptance proves only the constraints of that
supplied matrix, not those of an intended external code. Authenticating the matrix or
channel observations is outside this model. On inputs containing visible ﬂips, compatibility
is an algebraic property; the BEC likelihood theorem is not a guarantee about the physical
channel that generated them.
5. Representation, verification cost and certificate size
Assume a validated graph stored as check-to-variable and variable-to-check adjacency lists
with no duplicate incidences. Use a word-RAM with words large enough for indices and
counters, constant-time binary arithmetic and array access. Graph validation and construc-
tion from a dense input matrix require Θ(mn) entry inspections; they are separate from the
following per-input bounds. Storing a matrix densely does not become a sparse algorithm
merely by calling a pivot heuristic “sparse. ”
Proposition 5.1 (Sparse checking upper bound) . A success or failure certiﬁcate can be
checked in O(n + m +jEj) word operations and O(n + m +jEj) space, including graph
storage. Direct veriﬁcation of a binary full word against H and y has the same asymptotic
upper bound and requires no trace.
Proof. Reading y and initializing variable status costs O(n); initializing check arrays costs
O(m). Visiting each incidence once computes syndromes, residual degrees and the XOR of
the indices of each check’s unknown neighbors, at cost O(jEj). When a residual degree is
one, this last XOR identiﬁes its unique unknown index in constant time, even if that index
is zero. The checker validates each triple using these arrays, then visits the adjacency list

additionally checks that every declared local assignment is justiﬁed by a degree-one check
in the preceding operational state. It therefore certiﬁes a local operational explanation, not
a stronger ﬁnal-word correctness predicate. This explanation concerns the supplied trace,
not the producer’s actual internal history. For an empty trace, a failure witness for the
initial erasure system already certiﬁes impossibility without any peeling history.
Certiﬁcate construction has a diﬀerent cost. A straightforward dense elimination of

storage is O(q +m(q +m)) bits apart from indexing, including the returned solution. Packed
machine-word rows can improve constants and word-operation bounds. Peeling may reduce
q, but no universal speedup over GE-only follows, and recording the row transformations can
itself be expensive. Our comparisons use the same algebraic engine to keep that distinction
observable.
6. SELL-style rewriting and asynchronous adequacy
The principal result of this section is Theorem 6.10: an exact correspondence between
asynchronous rewrite events and operational peeling traces. A Solve event may occur while
other checks still await values assigned by earlier events. The stored rewrite residuals
therefore cannot be identiﬁed directly with the fully updated operational residuals. We
relate them through an invariant on pending incidences, then use propagation normalization
to obtain an exact endpoint that retains every log atom.
The two rules use persistent knowledge and linear residual resources. Their semantics
here is multiset rewriting; the logical notation presents an access discipline rather than

6.4. What the subexponential labels contribute. Following the resource distinction of
linear logic and its subexponential extension [ Gir87, NM09], one possible logical vocabulary
uses the subexponential signature Σ = (fS, Kg,,fS, Kg), where is the discrete preorder
and both labels admit weakening and contraction. The S zone contains static edge facts
and reusable program clauses; the K zone contains learned value facts. Linear atoms have
no such preﬁx. The displayed clauses can be read as ﬁrst-order multiplicative formulas,
with ﬁnite variable and check sorts, natural-number degree indices, and F2-valued indices.
Equivalently, use their ground instances, interpreting successor and XOR in these index
domains. This speciﬁes the intended clause syntax and structural permissions. In this paper
the executable meaning is exactly the two operational rules already deﬁned. Structural proof
rules are not extra transitions of that operational system.
The subexponential presentation exposes the intended access discipline: static data and
reusable clauses occupy S, while newly learned values occupy K. The following proposition
identiﬁes a distinction that is semantically inessential for this transition system. It gives
a controlled comparison with one persistent zone and with ordinary multiset rewriting,
preserving the events used by the main theorem.

Proposition 6.12 separates the persistent-zone vocabulary from the operational obliga-

by the abstract principle are invariant preservation, projection and log behavior, canonical
silent normalization, and local lifting. Their use here does not depend on a comparison
between subexponential labels.
Diﬀerentiated persistent zones thus supply a speciﬁcation discipline, without an increase
in operational expressiveness for these rules. The proposition concerns the two stated
rewrite systems, not an equivalence between full logical calculi or a minimality theorem for
resource-sensitive representations. In particular, it does not establish adequacy for a focused
calculus of the kind studied in [ And92, NM09]. The certiﬁcation result remains the exact
event-labelled correspondence despite delayed propagation, with the log and normalization
conditions made explicit.

but there is no veriﬁed reﬁnement of those Python caches.

8. Experimental methodology
The experiment measures the cost of checking a declared operational explanation alongside
the cost of direct ﬁnal-word veriﬁcation. It evaluates a Python reference implementation,
not code extracted from Lean. All data discussed here were generated by a new, prespeciﬁed
campaign on 23 September 2026. The earlier manuscript’s outputs remain in a separate,
unchanged directory and are not used in these ﬁgures or tables. The conﬁguration, individ-
ual matrix adjacency lists and hashes, random seeds, per-trial outcomes, repeated timings,
source snapshots and environment metadata accompany the artifact.
Graphs and observations. Both constructions have variable degree three and check de-
gree six, so m = n/2. The regular construction samples uniform socket permutations and
rejects nonsimple graphs. The PEG variant chooses maximum graph distance and then
minimum check degree, subject to a check-capacity constraint, and restarts if a construc-
tion gets stuck. It is an explicitly speciﬁed variant of the progressive edge-growth method
[HEA05]; we do not assume it samples a uniform ensemble or reproduces another imple-
mentation’s ﬁnite-length performance. Each family uses n2f 128, 256, 512, 1024g and three
independently seeded matrices per size, giving 24 matrices. Each matrix is reused across
the channel conditions. Matrix generation, validation, dense conversion and kernel-basis
construction are measured once per matrix and excluded from per-observation decoding
times.
The clean campaign uses ε2f 0.30, 0.40, 0.50g and ten observations per matrix and
erasure probability, giving 720 trials. For each trial, a uniformly sampled coeﬃcient vector in
a computed kernel basis generates a uniformly sampled codeword. Erasures are independent
Bernoulli variables. Distinct random streams generate the codeword, erasures, visible ﬂips
and measurement order. Their seeds are 64-bit preﬁxes of SHA-256 hashes of a ﬁxed base
seed and the complete trial/stream labels; the artifact records the exact encoding and checks
for collisions in the executed grid.
Corruption trials use both families, n2f 256, 512g, ε2f 0.40, 0.55g and ﬂip probabilities
δ2f 0.001, 0.01g, with three matrices and ten observations per condition, giving 480 trials.
A ﬂip applies only at a nonerased coordinate. We record the realized number of ﬂips,
since a positive δ does not imply any bit changed. Compatibility means existence of a
codeword consistent with the supplied, possibly corrupted, observation. Incompatibility is
accompanied by a checked separator.
Compared operations and timing. We measure peeling only, Gaussian elimination only,
hybrid decoding, hybrid decoding with certiﬁcate checking, and independent direct word
veriﬁcation. All algebraic pipelines use the same integer-bitset, ﬁrst-available-pivot elimina-
tion engine and row-transformation tracking. The hybrid without checking still constructs
its certiﬁcate; the comparison isolates execution of the checker and does not measure a
producer optimized to avoid witness construction. Peeling alone is incomplete on stopping
sets. Direct veriﬁcation receives an already produced word and is a veriﬁcation operation,
not a decoder or an infeasibility test.

Each per-observation operation includes its own required initialization on a prevalidated
adjacency structure. There is one warmup per operation and three timed repetitions in de-
terministically randomized order. Comparator times are within-trial medians. The certiﬁed
pipeline also records initialization, peeling, residual construction, elimination, witness ex-
traction, back substitution, other producer work and checking. For its stage decomposition
we select the actual repetition with median internal total time; this preserves the identity
Ttotal = Tdecode + Tcheck.
Here Tdecode includes construction of the certiﬁcate. The internal Tcheck also includes the
producer wrapper’s assertion that the checker returns its proposed word; standalone replay
timings exclude that wrapper comparison. We report both Rrelative = Tcheck/Tdecode and
Rfraction = Tcheck/Ttotal. A mean of per-trial ratios is not a ratio of mean times; the condition-
summary CSV provides both. The outer comparator timer also measures call overhead, so
it is not silently identiﬁed with an internal stage timer.
A separate checker experiment uses 15 additional regular matrices: three each at n2
f256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096g, six observations per matrix, ε = 0 .40, and ﬁve randomized
timing repetitions. These 90 trials use the all-zero transmitted word to isolate checking; no
recovery inference is drawn from them. Each checker is called once for validation before
timing. Sparse replay, dense replay and direct checking receive the same instance and
resulting completion; the two replay checkers receive the same certiﬁcate. Dense input
conversion is excluded, but the dense checker scans its matrix during checking.
Memory is measured separately using tracemalloc: peak incremental Python-tracked
allocation during a call, not resident set size or total artifact storage. One observation per
matrix and clean channel condition, and one per scaling matrix, is proﬁled. The preex-
isting graph, dense matrix and certiﬁcate are outside that incremental measurement. The
run used an Apple M3 Pro (11 reported logical CPUs, approximately 18 GiB RAM), ma-
cOS 27.0 arm64, Python 3.12.14, NumPy 2.3.5, pandas 2.2.3 and Matplotlib 3.10.7, with
perf_counter as the clock. Exact package versions are pinned.
Outcomes and uncertainty . Completion means acceptance of a success certiﬁcate. Ex-
act recovery compares its word with the sampled transmitted word. Uniqueness is jUj 
rank(HU ) = 0 , conditional on compatibility. These meanings are those of Section 3. Failure-
certiﬁcate acceptance is recorded separately from completion. Each displayed condition at a
ﬁxed family, size and channel parameter has three matrix realizations and ten observations
per matrix. Intervals in the supplementary data use 2,000 bootstrap resamples of matrix
means, retaining within-matrix dependence. The scaling ﬁt is ordinary least squares of log
mean time against log blocklength; its interval resamples the three matrix means indepen-
dently within each size. These descriptive intervals have limited ensemble resolution: three
matrices do not support strong distributional claims, even when many channel observations
or timing repetitions are available.

10. Related work
The decoding and algebraic foundations are established. Gallager introduced LDPC codes
[Gal62]. Iterative erasure correction is developed in [ LMSS01], and ﬁnite-length stopping-
set analysis in [ DPT+02]. Burshtein and Miller combine iterative decoding with algebraic
methods for BEC maximum-likelihood decoding [ BM04]. These results supply the algorith-
mic setting of the present paper. Our residual bijection and ﬁnite-ﬁeld alternative articulate
the success/failure certiﬁcate contract for that setting rather than introducing a new hybrid
decoder or channel threshold.
Certifying algorithms separate producing a result from checking its witness [ MMNS11].
Alkassar and coauthors connect high-level witness properties with veriﬁed checker implemen-
tations [ ABMR13]; proof-carrying code supplies a related division for code-safety evidence
[Nec97]. We apply this producer/checker organization to a declared local trace and its resid-
ual result. Linear-programming decoding also connects decoding to optimization guarantees
[FWK05]. Its relaxation-based optimality setting diﬀers from the BEC feasibility predicate
and local trace explanation checked here.
Linear logic provides the distinction between consumable and reusable resources [ Gir87],
and focusing structures proof search [ And92]. Nigam and Miller study algorithmic speci-
ﬁcations using subexponential mechanisms richer than the two persistent zones used here
[NM09]. Our operational resource presentation uses this vocabulary to expose pending work
and reusable knowledge. Proposition 6.12 then identiﬁes the label distinction as inessen-
tial to these particular transitions. This representation result complements Theorem 6.10;
neither is a correspondence theorem for a SELL sequent calculus or focused proof search.
Formal coding theory supplies a closer algorithmic antecedent as well. Aﬀeldt, Garrigue
and Saikawa formalize linear error-correcting codes in Coq, including a BEC sum-product

PAGE 26
26 C. RAMÍREZ OV ALLE
decoder with a terminating ﬁxed-point construction and approximation invariants, and
extract an LDPC sum-product implementation [ AGS20]. Our emphasis is the exact chrono-
logical event trace of delayed propagation, its logged normal form, and the success/failure
witnesses for the residual system. The canonical Lean development formalizes this speciﬁc
correspondence with an explicit boundary to raw multisets and independent Python code.
Recent work illustrates other certiﬁcation targets in coding theory. Ehatamm and coau-
thors develop Lean-based quantum-code distance certiﬁcates through veriﬁed reductions to
satisﬁability [ EL WT26]. Krishnamoorthy and coauthors study certiﬁcates for degenerate
quantum-decoding optimality using partition-function comparisons [ KGK+26]. These 2026
preprints concern distance and comparison of quantum error classes, respectively. Their
target properties diﬀer from the BEC trace/feasibility contract.
Relative to these cited developments, the speciﬁc issue addressed here is the connection
between an asynchronously maintained residual state and an exact, independently checkable
operational explanation. Theorem 6.10 supplies that connection for arbitrary interleavings,
and the residual certiﬁcate theorems extend it to either a compatible completion or an
infeasibility witness. This is the positive scope of the contribution assessed against the
reviewed literature; we make no ﬁeld-wide priority claim for its combination or for the
abstract simulation principle used to organize its proof.
11. Conclusion and limitations
Delayed propagation separates the stored residual resources of an asynchronous decoder
speciﬁcation from its actual operational state. The pending-incidence invariant quantiﬁes
this separation, and propagation normalization recovers the unique fully updated context.
The principal result, Theorem 6.10, proves exact correspondence in both directions: arbi-
trary rewrite interleavings emit valid operational traces, and each prescribed valid trace has
a realization with its precise logged normalized endpoint. The abstract proposition isolates
the forward simulation, normalization and local lifting on which this result depends; empty
and partial traces require no maximality assumption.
The residual bijection connects this trace semantics to a complete success/failure con-
tract. A residual solution supplies a compatible codeword; a left-kernel separator excludes
every compatible word. The Lean development proves the canonical LDPC correspondence
and the stated certiﬁcate results, while ﬁnite tests and experiments assess an independent
executable implementation. The representation table in Section 7 marks the mathematical,
mechanized and tested connections separately.
The SELL-style presentation exposes reusable knowledge and consumable pending work.
Proposition 6.12 shows that its two persistent labels can be merged for these rules, thereby
separating a useful speciﬁcation discipline from what the operational proof needs. No
SELLF adequacy or expressiveness separation follows.
Trace replay certiﬁes a declared local operational explanation, not the producer’s true
internal history. Direct checking establishes the same ﬁnal-word validity condition and
was faster in the retained measurements. A compatible completion need not be unique or
equal the transmitted word; visible corruption can remain algebraically compatible. The
supplied matrix, observation, checker and execution environment remain within the stated
trust boundary. Python is not extracted from Lean, and the abstract proposition and
raw-representation bridge are manuscript proofs.
